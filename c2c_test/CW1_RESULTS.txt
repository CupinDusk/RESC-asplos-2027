Structural precondition CONFIRMED in every completed cell:
  arm2 readshare_served = 0 (arm2 drains EVERY lagged read at LAG=2),
  arm3 readshare_served > 0 (13440..860160), readshare_expired = 0,
  BITIDENTICAL = 1 for arm2 AND arm3 in every cell (timing-only).

H1 (concurrency / queueing) -- does NOT bind in the ring's favour:
  arm2 L2 read path stays L2-HIT-resident and cheap. At nc=64,
  L2_cache_stats_breakdown[GLOBAL_ACC_R]: arm2 HIT=243708 MISS=1;
  arm2 L2_total_cache_miss_rate flat at 0.533 across nc=4..128;
  total dram reads = 1 (no DRAM). arm2's drain is an L2 HIT at
  ~242cyc ROP latency, essentially independent of cluster count.
  Meanwhile averagemflatency rises with scale for BOTH arms
  (arm2: 336->487->1005->1602; arm3: 360->623->1363->2603) and is
  ALWAYS HIGHER for arm3. The SM-to-SM fabric serve (195cyc nominal)
  congests WORSE under concurrency than the shared-L2 hit it replaces.
  => H1 queueing does not make the L2 fallback more expensive than the
     fabric; the fabric is the more-congested resource. Latency-bound.

H2 (capacity miss to DRAM) -- GENUINELY appeared at tl=16 and STILL
did not rescue the ring (the decisive point):
  At tile_lines=16 the working set (~33 MB) exceeds the ~4 MB L2, so
  arm2's drains really DO miss to DRAM as H2 predicted:
     arm2 tl16: L2_total_cache_miss_rate = 0.8716,
                total dram reads = 680905, L2 GLOBAL_ACC_R MISS = 170227.
  This is exactly the "arm2 drains to DRAM at 466cyc" regime the
  hypothesis said would make the ring win. It did not:
     arm3 tl16 = 254989 cyc vs arm2 tl16 = 241523 cyc => 1.056 (ring
     5.6% SLOWER).
  Decisively, arm3 tl16 served ALL 860160 reads from peer L1:
     arm3 tl16: total dram reads = 1, L2 GLOBAL_ACC_R MISS = 1.
  So arm3 touches DRAM ZERO times and is STILL slower than arm2 which
  issues 680905 DRAM reads. => arm2 is NOT DRAM-latency-bound even when
  it misses to DRAM: the drained reads are pipelined/overlapped through
  the 64 partition queues faster than the ring's peer-L1 fabric can
  serve them one lease at a time. Growing WS further (tl=64/256) cannot
  invert a fallback that already beats a zero-DRAM competitor.

CONCLUSION: In this sim the DeNovo/hLRC single-interval baseline (arm2)
is not the bottleneck the ring was supposed to relieve. Neither L2 queue
saturation (H1) nor capacity-miss-to-DRAM (H2) makes arm2's fallback
more expensive than the ring's SM-to-SM fabric serve. This is the
contention-latency-bound sim-fidelity boundary anticipated in the
pre-registration and in prior memory
(clustersim-contention-latency-bound-blocker). Reported as-is; no
gerrymandering; the workload is the honest pipe_chain distributed
fan-in=1 regime (NOT a single-producer funnel).
